Selection procedure and enrolment

MSP Admissions Board
The three selection criteria
01MSP

Motivation letter assessment

The Admissions Board reads your letter first.

Tap to see the motivation letter questions →

The motivation letter is, in addition to your previous academic performance, a very important part of the admissions process and will be used as a starting point for the interview. You write it in the MSP format in the MyApplication portal; letters in other formats are not taken into account.

  1. Please indicate the sources from which you learned about MSP and its programme.Tick boxes
  2. Please indicate your specific academic interests or passions by ticking the relevant boxes below. You can choose multiple options if you have more than one academic interest.Biology, Chemistry, Geoscience, Physics, Neuroscience, Mathematics, Programming
  3. Briefly explain why these fields of study motivate you to apply to the Maastricht Science Programme.max 150 words
  4. Please select a minimum of 3 and a maximum of 5 elective courses from the course catalogue (no core courses, skills or projects) that interest you the most. Please provide a brief (collective) explanation for why you have chosen these particular courses.max 100 words excluding course titles
  5. Why do you want to study at MSP specifically?max 150 words
  6. Please list any other university programmes you have considered or applied for.
  7. If you could research any scientific topic without any funding or resource limitations, what would you research and why?max 150 words
  8. Envision your life post-graduation. What are your immediate plans upon graduating, and what academic and personal achievements do you aspire to have accomplished five years after graduation?max 75 words
02MSP

Maths and science focus review

Your background in mathematics and the sciences you want to combine.

Tap for the maths background →

MSP is a broad science programme, so a profound interest in the sciences is required. A mathematics background that includes calculus, specifically the fundamentals of differentiation and integration, is highly recommended (equivalent to Dutch VWO Wiskunde B). The MSP Admissions Office may ask for additional tests proving a high level in mathematics, specifically calculus, integration and differentiation.

03MSP

Online interview with a staff member

A personal conversation, held online, when you are invited.

Tap for how to prepare →

If you are invited, you have a personal interview, online, in English, with one MSP staff member. It takes about 10-15 minutes and focuses on your academic plans and how you intend to accomplish these through an education at MSP.

The selection procedure, including the interview, closes on 28 May 2027, 17:00 CET

How to prepare

  • Know the programme and Problem-Based Learning.
  • Review your own application.
  • Be able to articulate your scientific passion.
  • Show your personal fit.

FAQ

Frequently asked

Diploma, mathematics, more than one programme, the fee.

Tap for the answers →
Can I apply without my certified diploma?

Yes. The central Student Services Centre and the MSP Admissions Office guide you together. You start in Studielink, which opens on 1 October 2026, and upload the certified copy once you have it, before 31 August.

Is mathematics required?

MSP is a broad science programme, so a profound interest in the sciences is required. A mathematics background that includes calculus, specifically the fundamentals of differentiation and integration, is highly recommended (equivalent to Dutch VWO Wiskunde B). The MSP Admissions Office may ask for additional tests proving a high level in mathematics, specifically calculus, integration and differentiation.

Can I apply for more bachelor's programmes at the same time?

Yes. You can apply for several programmes at Maastricht University at once. Through Studielink you may have at most four active programme choices across all Dutch universities at the same time.

Do I have to pay an administrative fee?

That depends on your curriculum. Applicants whose diploma is based on a non-EU/EEA curriculum pay a handling fee. This follows the school's curriculum, not where the school is located.
